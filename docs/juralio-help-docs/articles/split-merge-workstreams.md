# Split and merge workstreams

## Splitting a workstream

Sometimes, matter or workstreams grow so many tasks that they become unwieldy. Or specialist elements distract everyone else.

You can address this by creating a new workstream and moving some tasks into it, thereby splitting the workstream.

* This needn't take long, as subtasks move with their parents.
* Often, moving one or two carefully-selected phases or level B tasks with many subtasks will do the job

On creating a new workstream, you will be offered a convenient opportunity to populate it with tasks from an existing workstream. However, you can choose whether to do so at that point or to wait and split out the tasks later, using the approach discussed above.

## Merging workstreams

Sometimes, workstreams turn out smaller than expected.

* In that case, you can move tasks from one workstream into another, and close the first workstream.
