# Examples of how to break down tasks

## M&A example

* **Phases** might include Heads of Terms, Due Diligence, SPA Negotiation, Completion, Post-Completion.
* **Level B** subtasks within Due Diligence might cover different categories of diligence (e.g. Financial, Legal, Technical).
* Further breakdowns are likely to be context-specific.

## Litigation example

* **Phases** in English litigation might include Statements of Case, Disclosure, Witness Statements, Expert Reports and Trial.
  * It may also be sensible - though this is for the team to decide - to have some phases for things handled throughout the workstream, e.g. advice
  * Other phases may also be required depending on context, e.g. if there is a jurisdiction challenge or a summary judgment application
  * Just to illustrate how a task might be broken down further -
    * **Level B** subtasks of **A. Witness Statements** might be structured by reference to different parties
    * **Level C** subtasks might then be structured by reference to particular witnesses called by each party
    * **Level D** subtasks might be created for each separate witness statement of each witness
    * **Level E** subtasks might be used for the separate sections of each witness statement if complex
    * **Level F** subtasks might define specific issues arising within each section if very complex

## Arbitration example

* If the arbitration follows a process similar to common law litigation, similar phases may be appropriate
* If it follows a [memorial-style](http://blogs.lexisnexis.co.uk/dr/putting-the-case-for-memorials-in-international-arbitration/) then phases based around each round of memorials may be a better choice, with the different aspects of each memorial being reflected in the subtasks
* The important point is that the structure should be simple and reflect the way people will naturally work on the arbitration
